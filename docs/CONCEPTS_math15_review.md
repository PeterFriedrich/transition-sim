# Math 15 review: concept list (integers, BEDMAS, fractions, decimals)

Format per concept: ID | idea | rules/procedure | worked examples | common errors | prerequisites

---

## 1. INTEGERS

### 1.1 Meaning of integers
- Idea: whole numbers with direction. Sign = direction, absolute value = distance from 0.
- Use the number line, not memorized rules.
- Prereqs: none

### 1.2 Adding integers
- Same signs: add the sizes, keep the sign.
- Different signs: subtract the sizes, keep the sign of the larger size.
- Examples: (-4) + (-3) = -7; (-9) + 5 = -4; 9 + (-5) = 4
- Errors: applying "two negatives make a positive" to addition ((-4) + (-3) is not 7)
- Prereqs: 1.1

### 1.3 Subtracting integers
- Subtracting = adding the opposite.
- Examples: 5 - (-3) = 5 + 3 = 8; (-5) - 3 = -8; (-5) - (-3) = -2
- Why: taking away a debt of 3 leaves you 3 higher.
- Errors: treating "-" as an operation and as a sign interchangeably (5 - -3 written as 5 - 3)
- Prereqs: 1.2

### 1.4 Multiplying and dividing integers
- Same signs: positive. Different signs: negative.
- Examples: (-6)(-4) = 24; (-6)(4) = -24; (-12) ÷ (-3) = 4; 12 ÷ (-3) = -4
- Why: pattern 3(-2) = -6, 2(-2) = -4, 1(-2) = -2, 0(-2) = 0, so (-1)(-2) = +2.
- Errors: dropping the sign after multiplying
- Prereqs: 1.1

### 1.5 Integers with exponents and brackets
- The exponent applies only to what is directly attached.
- Examples: (-3)^2 = 9; -3^2 = -9; -2(3 - 7) = -2(-4) = 8
- Prereqs: 1.4, 2.1

### 1.6 Quick check (answers in brackets)
1. (-7) + 10 [3]
2. (-8) - (-5) [-3]
3. (-3)(-4)(-2) [-24]
4. 6 - 2(-3) [12]
5. (-18) ÷ 3 - (-4) [-2]
6. -4^2 and (-4)^2 [-16 and 16]

## 2. BEDMAS (ORDER OF OPERATIONS)

### 2.1 The order
1. Brackets
2. Exponents
3. Division and Multiplication, left to right (same priority)
4. Addition and Subtraction, left to right (same priority)
- Canadian name for PEMDAS (US).
- Brackets include anything grouped: a numerator or denominator under a fraction bar, the inside of a root.
- Errors: "multiplication before division" (D and M are tied; A and S are tied)
- Examples: 12 ÷ 3 × 2 = 4 × 2 = 8 (not 2)
- Prereqs: arithmetic facts

### 2.2 Worked example
- 8 - 2(3 + 1)^2 ÷ 4
- Brackets: 8 - 2(4)^2 ÷ 4
- Exponent: 8 - 2(16) ÷ 4
- Multiply then divide, left to right: 8 - 32 ÷ 4 = 8 - 8
- Subtract: 0

### 2.3 With integers
- -5 - (-3) × 2: multiply first -> -5 - (-6) = -5 + 6 = 1
- Prereqs: 1.3, 1.4, 2.1

## 3. FRACTIONS

### 3.1 Meaning
- a/b means a pieces of size 1/b. It is one number with a location on the number line, not two separate numbers.
- Fraction magnitude understanding is a major predictor of later algebra success.
- Prereqs: division facts

### 3.2 Equivalent fractions and simplifying
- Multiply or divide top and bottom by the same number: 2/3 = 4/6 = 10/15
- Simplify by dividing by the GCF: 18/24 ÷ 6 -> 3/4
- Errors: simplifying only part of the answer; not simplifying
- Prereqs: factors, GCF, multiplication facts

### 3.3 Comparing and ordering
- Use a common denominator or benchmarks (0, 1/2, 1).
- Example: 3/5, 2/3, 5/8 = 0.6, 0.667, 0.625 -> 3/5 < 5/8 < 2/3
- Shortcut: same numerator, bigger denominator means smaller fraction (1/3 > 1/5).
- Prereqs: 3.2

### 3.4 Adding and subtracting
- Needs a common denominator (use the LCM). Only numerators combine.
- Examples: 2/3 + 3/4 = 8/12 + 9/12 = 17/12 = 1 5/12; 5/6 - 1/4 = 10/12 - 3/12 = 7/12
- Errors: adding tops and bottoms (2/3 + 3/4 = 5/7 is wrong)
- Prereqs: 3.2, LCM

### 3.5 Multiplying
- Straight across; cancel first to keep numbers small.
- Example: 2/3 × 9/10 = 18/30 = 3/5
- Meaning: 2/3 of 9/10.
- Errors: finding a common denominator (not needed)
- Prereqs: 3.2

### 3.6 Dividing
- Multiply by the reciprocal.
- Example: 3/4 ÷ 2/3 = 3/4 × 3/2 = 9/8
- Why: asks how many 2/3s fit in 3/4; (2/3)(3/2) = 1.
- Sanity check: 2/3 is slightly smaller than 3/4, so the answer is a bit over 1.
- Errors: flipping the wrong fraction
- Prereqs: 3.5

### 3.7 Mixed numbers
- Convert to improper before multiplying or dividing.
- Example: 2 1/2 × 1 1/3 = 5/2 × 4/3 = 20/6 = 10/3 = 3 1/3
- For add/subtract, whole parts can be kept separate, but improper is safer than borrowing.
- Prereqs: 3.4, 3.5, 3.6

### 3.8 Fractions with integers
- Same sign rules.
- Examples: (-2/3) × (3/4) = -1/2; -3/5 ÷ (-1/10) = -3/5 × -10/1 = 6
- Prereqs: 1.4, 3.5, 3.6

### 3.9 Quick check (answers in brackets)

2. Order 3/4, 5/7, 2/3 [2/3 < 5/7 < 3/4]
3. 1/2 + 3/5 [11/10]
4. 7/8 - 1/3 [13/24]

6. 5/6 ÷ 5/12 [2]
7. 1 1/2 ÷ 3/4 [2]
8. (-2/5) - (-1/10) [-3/10]

## 4. DECIMALS

### 4.1 Meaning and place value
- Decimals are fractions with denominators 10, 100, 1000...
- Places to the right of the point: tenths, hundredths, thousandths.
- 0.25 = 25/100 = 1/4. 0.5 and 0.50 are the same value.
- Prereqs: place value

### 4.2 Comparing and ordering
- Compare digit by digit from the left after padding with zeros.
- Example: 0.3, 0.25, 0.305 -> 0.300, 0.250, 0.305 -> 0.25 < 0.3 < 0.305
- Errors: thinking 0.25 > 0.3 because 25 > 3; more digits means bigger
- Prereqs: 4.1

### 4.3 Adding and subtracting (column method)
- Also called vertical addition/subtraction or the standard algorithm.
- Line up decimal points, pad with zeros, work right to left.
- Carrying (regrouping) in addition; borrowing (regrouping) in subtraction.
- Examples: 4.7 + 0.35 = 4.70 + 0.35 = 5.05; 3 - 0.84 = 3.00 - 0.84 = 2.16
- Errors: not lining up the points
- Prereqs: 4.1

### 4.4 Multiplying
- Multiply as whole numbers, then give the answer as many decimal places as both factors combined.
- Example: 0.3 × 0.04 -> 3 × 4 = 12 -> 3 places -> 0.012
- Sanity check: the result should be smaller than both factors.
- Errors: wrong decimal placement
- Prereqs: 4.1

### 4.5 Dividing
- Shift the point in both numbers until the divisor is whole (same shift on each).
- Examples: 4.5 ÷ 0.3 = 45 ÷ 3 = 15; 0.72 ÷ 0.06 = 72 ÷ 6 = 12
- Why: equivalent fractions (multiply top and bottom by 10 or 100).
- Errors: moving the point in only the divisor
- Prereqs: 4.4

### 4.6 Multiplying and dividing by powers of 10
- Move the point; do not "add zeros."
- Examples: 3.7 × 100 = 370; 52 ÷ 1000 = 0.052

### 4.7 Fraction to decimal (long division)
- Divide top by bottom. If the numerator is smaller, add a decimal point and zeros to it.
- Put the decimal point in the answer directly above the point in the dividend, then ignore it while dividing as whole numbers.
- Example: 3/8 -> 3.000 ÷ 8: 30 ÷ 8 = 3 r6; 60 ÷ 8 = 7 r4; 40 ÷ 8 = 5 r0 -> 0.375
- Terminating: remainder reaches 0.
- Repeating: a remainder repeats. For 1 ÷ 3: 10 ÷ 3 = 3 r1, bring down 0, get 10 again, remainder 1 keeps recurring, digit 3 repeats -> 0.333...
- With real decimals: 7.5 ÷ 5 -> point above the point, 75 ÷ 5 = 15 -> 1.5
- Prereqs: 3.1, 4.5

### 4.8 Decimal to fraction
- Read the place value, then simplify.
- Examples: 0.35 = 35/100 = 7/20; 0.045 = 45/1000 = 9/200
- Prereqs: 3.2, 4.1

### 4.9 Benchmark conversions to know cold
- 1/2 = 0.5; 1/4 = 0.25; 3/4 = 0.75; 1/5 = 0.2; 1/8 = 0.125; 1/3 = 0.333... ; 2/3 = 0.666...
- A simplified fraction terminates only if its denominator has just factors of 2 and 5. That is why 1/3 repeats and 1/8 does not.

### 4.10 Rounding
- Look at the digit just past the one you keep. 5 or more rounds up.
- Examples: 3.746 to 2 decimals -> 3.75; 0.0849 to 2 decimals -> 0.08
- Round only at the end of a multi-step calculation.
- Errors: rounding at each step

### 4.11 Decimals with negatives
- Same integer sign rules.
- Examples: (-0.4)(0.5) = -0.2; -1.2 ÷ (-0.04) = 30
- Prereqs: 1.4, 4.4, 4.5

### 4.12 Quick check (answers in brackets)
1. Order 0.4, 0.045, 0.39, 0.4005 [0.045 < 0.39 < 0.4 < 0.4005]
2. 6.2 - 3.75 [2.45]
3. 0.6 × 0.07 [0.042]
4. 2.4 ÷ 0.08 [30]
5. 5/16 as a decimal [0.3125]
6. 0.045 as a simplified fraction [9/200]
7. Round 12.0963 to 2 decimals [12.10]
8. (-0.8) × 1.5 + 2 [0.8]
